# 23. Judge Cross-Examination

> **Status:** Living document
> **Version:** 1.0
> **Last Updated:** 2026-09-27
> **Rule:** answer 1–3 sentences, then land one concrete fact from the code/tests.**

---

## Q1. "What's actually new here? This is just watermarking plus a database."

> "Every primitive is standard — that is deliberate. The contribution is the offline chain of
> evidence: a per-session 128-bit opaque fingerprint derived from doc+recipient+session+nonce,
> a recipient ML-DSA signature over the event, and a ledger where **each node independently
> signs entries and Merkle checkpoints with its own key**. There is no single server that can
> forge the record. It is validated by `test_admin_without_node_keys_cannot_rewrite_all_nodes`."

## Q2. "Why not use Hyperledger Fabric / a real blockchain?"

> "Fabric adds operational weight without adding anything the PS asks for. We need
> tamper-evidence and no-single-admin on an air-gapped LAN, and we get it structurally: each
> node key is generated on and never leaves its device. The PS explicitly allows the simpler
> append-only ledger as the prototype fallback. We call it a DLT-style signed ledger, not a
> consensus blockchain."

## Q3. "Can't an admin just delete the log files?"

> "In **local** mode, yes, an attacker with both the files and the node key files could. That
> is why LAN mode exists: the four nodes become four devices, each holding its own private
> key, and a quorum of them must sign. Then a privileged coordinator cannot forge history.
> We state the local-mode caveat openly in the README."

## Q4. "Does the watermark survive screenshots or printing?"

> "Not guaranteed, and we never claim it. The PS itself flags this as a fundamental limit.
> We ship a `rasterize` mode for stronger full-page robustness and we ran JPEG re-render tests
> that pass, but a camera photo is future work, not a claim."

## Q5. "What if the recipient just removes the watermark?"

> "Then attribution may fail — that is a stated limitation. Our channels are robust to normal
> PDF copies/re-saves and JPEG re-encoding of embedded images, which is the realistic threat
> for document leaks. We explicitly say 'forensically robust against selected transformations',
> not 'unremovable'."

## Q6. "The server signs with the recipient's key — how is that non-repudiation?"

> "It's a genuine weakness and we say so. After unlock, the server holds the key for 30
> minutes and signs on the recipient's behalf. True non-repudiation needs client-side signing
> (liboqs/WASM or a local key agent) or a hardware token. The architecture — canonical event,
> per-recipient key, public key stored with the entry — is ready for that change."

## Q7. "Can an attacker forge a watermark ID to frame someone?"

> "No. The ID is a 128-bit hash; guessing is 2¹²⁸. More importantly, a watermark not present
> in the ledger returns `NO_LEDGER_MATCH`, and a present-but-modified event fails ML-DSA
> verification. Both cases are tested (`fake-watermark`, `signature-tampering`)."

## Q8. "Is the watermark unique per recipient per session?"

> "Yes. It is `SHA-256(document_hash | recipient | session | nonce)` truncated to 128 bits.
> Same recipient, second decryption → different fingerprint. Two recipients → different
> fingerprints. `test_watermark_generation_uniqueness` proves it."

## Q9. "What stops the recipient from decrypting and sharing before watermarking?"

> "Nothing, if they control the endpoint — the PS says so. The watermark is embedded as part
> of the decryption path we control, and the ledger records the session. A modified viewer is
> an endpoint trust problem; the future mitigation is a hardened viewer plus hardware-backed
> keys."

## Q10. "Is this quantum-safe?"

> "Key establishment is ML-KEM-768 and signatures are ML-DSA-65 — both NIST standards
> (FIPS 203/204), implemented via `pqcrypto` on liboqs/PQClean, not hand-rolled. The
> data cipher is AES-256-GCM. Everything fails loudly if the PQC library is missing."

## Q11. "Why is the session ID so short (6 hex)?"

> "It's a human-readable label. The actual security comes from the 128-bit random nonce that
> feeds the watermark hash. The session ID alone can never be used to derive or guess a
> watermark."

## Q12. "Prove the demo wasn't pre-recorded."

> "Run `POST /api/test/end-to-end` live. It generates a fresh PDF, encrypts it, lets ALICE and
> BOB decrypt, leaks Alice's copy, verifies it, and returns per-check booleans. Or register a
> brand-new account and repeat the flow."

## Q13. "What's the false-positive/false-negative story?"

> "False attribution would require (a) forging a 128-bit hash into the ledger, or (b) forging
> a recipient signature, or (c) a quorum of node keys — all out of the threat model. Extreme
> robustness edge cases are acknowledged: heavy watermark destruction yields `NO_WATERMARK`,
> not a wrong name, which is the safe failure direction."

## Q14. "How does this scale?"

> "The prototype is deliberately file-based. At scale the coordinator storage moves to a
> service + object store, ledger append becomes O(log n) with incremental Merkle updates, and
> witness nodes stay as independent signers. The trust architecture does not change."

## Q15. "What would you do with 6 more months?"

> "Client-side/hardware signing for real non-repudiation; a hardened viewer with TEE where
> available; camera/print-scan robustness experiments with published rates; an independent
> security audit; and TLS with a local CA for the LAN."

## Q16. "Where is the AI/ML?"

> "There isn't any — and we made that choice on purpose. The problem is cryptographic
> evidence, not prediction. Calling it AI would be dishonest."

## Q17. "What happens with one node down?"

> "The majority quorum still commits and attributes. `test_ledger_lan.py` proves it with four
> real processes: kill one and quorum still succeeds; corrupt one and attribution still
> resolves via the other valid nodes."

## Q18. "How is the ZIP fallback not a weakness?"

> "It is documented as protecting the wrapper only. If someone extracts the inner file and
> shares only that, the container watermark stays behind. We call that out rather than hide it.
> Native channels exist precisely so common document types don't depend on the fallback."

## Q19. "Why should a government/department buy this?"

> "For controlled sharing of classified or sensitive documents where leaks are investigated:
> fully offline on an isolated network, NIST PQC, no cloud dependency, and a verifiable audit
> trail that survives an insider with server access."

## Q20. "Give me the honest one-sentence limitations."

> "The server signs while keys are unlocked; camera/print-scan and deliberate watermark removal
> defeat attribution; the ledger is tamper-evident, not a BFT consensus; and the strong
> no-single-admin guarantee is the LAN mode, not the default local mode."
