## 1. The Real-World Situation

Organisations (government, defence, audit, legal, corporate) routinely share a single
sensitive document with several authorized people. The moment it is shared:

- the document exists as **many independent copies**,
- each copy is **byte-identical or visually identical**,
- and there is **no durable, trustworthy link** between a copy and the person holding it.

If a copy appears in the wrong place — a leak, a whistleblower dump, a competitor, a
court filing, a journalist — the natural question is:

> **Who leaked this copy?**

Today's answers are weak.

---

## 2. Why Existing Answers Fail

### 2.1 Server access logs

Logs record intent/action ("Alice downloaded at 10:30") but:

- a privileged operator can edit or delete them (the threat model explicitly includes
  "a privileged operator or compromised server account"),
- logs may be centralized and therefore a single point of forgery,
- logs do not travel with the leaked artifact — you must trust the server that kept them.

### 2.2 Static / visible watermarking

A watermark like `CONFIDENTIAL — PROJECT X` or the recipient's name:

- is the same for everyone (or is trivially cropped/overpainted),
- identifies the document, not the leaker,
- may be visually undesirable for official documents.

### 2.3 DRM and screen controls

DRM can restrict playback but:

- cannot survive a phone camera pointed at the screen,
- cannot survive print-and-scan,
- breaks legitimate workflows,
- is defeated by a determined endpoint attacker.

### 2.4 Forensic watermarking alone

Per-recipient invisible watermarking is a real, mature field. But watermarking alone:

- gives you an ID with no trustworthy registry,
- is only as reliable as the registry it is checked against,
- offers no cryptographic proof that the record was not altered.

### 2.5 Blockchains / DLT alone

A public blockchain gives tamper-evident records but:

- needs network access (conflicts with offline / air-gapped requirements),
- puts hashes on an external public system (privacy/governance concerns),
- is overkill and operationally awkward for a closed organisation.

---

## 3. The Information-to-Proof Gap

The core gap BOUND addresses:

```text
"We know who was AUTHORIZED to read it"
                  ≠
"We can PROVE which authorized session PRODUCED this leaked artifact"
```

Closing that gap requires three things simultaneously:

1. **Per-copy difference** that survives normal handling → *session-specific watermark*.
2. **A trustworthy record** that cannot be silently rewritten → *signed multi-node ledger*.
3. **A cryptographic link** between the record and the copy → *opaque watermark ID + ML-DSA signature*.

---

## 4. Why Post-Quantum Matters Here

BOUND's audit record is meant to stay trustworthy for years. Two cryptographic primitives
must survive the quantum transition:

- **Key establishment** — ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203) instead of RSA/ECDH.
- **Signatures** — ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) instead of RSA/ECDSA.

A "harvest now, decrypt later" adversary can record today's ciphertext and break classical
key exchange later. Using NIST-standard PQC now makes the record and the archive
quantum-resistant by design. This directly satisfies the "Post-Quantum" requirement in the
problem statement.

- ✅ **VALIDATED:** the prototype uses real `pqcrypto` (Rust bindings to liboqs/PQClean)
  for ML-KEM and ML-DSA; it fails loudly if the library is missing rather than silently
  falling back to RSA.

---

## 5. The Adversary We Actually Model

BOUND's declared threat model (README + PS):

1. A **privileged operator / compromised server account** tries to alter or delete the
   audit record.
2. A **recipient leaks a copy** and denies it.
3. An **investigator** must identify the source from an artifact.

Out of scope / explicitly not guaranteed:

- Stopping a leak.
- Stopping a camera.
- Surviving arbitrary watermark destruction.
- Proving a human physically acted.

---

## 6. Why Offline / Air-Gapped

For defence, intelligence, legal and government use, the document platform often runs on an
**isolated network with no uplink**. BOUND is designed so that:

- encryption, decryption, watermarking, signing and ledger commits all run locally,
- the ledger is replicated across **real devices on the isolated LAN**,
- each witness device's signing key never leaves that device.

This is what upgrades "an admin can't edit the log" from a policy claim to a structural one.

---

## 7. The One-Sentence Problem

> **A single leaked sensitive document usually cannot be tied to the specific authorized
> person and session that produced it, in a way that survives a privileged insider and
> works fully offline — BOUND makes that tie cryptographically verifiable.**
